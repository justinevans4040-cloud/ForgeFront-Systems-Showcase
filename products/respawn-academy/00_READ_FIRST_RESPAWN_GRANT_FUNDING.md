## 10. NSF PROJECT PITCH REQUIREMENTS

The Project Pitch is the immediate NSF gate.

Current NSF instructions require four elements:

1. Technology Innovation — up to 3,500 characters.
2. Technical Objectives and Challenges — up to 3,500 characters.
3. Market Opportunity — up to 1,750 characters.
4. Company and Team — up to 1,750 characters.

NSF explicitly says that merely describing product features or benefits is insufficient. The pitch must explain the core high-risk technical innovation, required R&D, competitive difference, technical challenges, market opportunity, and team suitability.

NSF currently states that a Project Pitch response typically takes approximately one to two months.

## 11. NSF PITCH LIMITS

Current NSF rules also impose submission limits.

A company or individual:

- may have only one Project Pitch under review at a time;
- may submit no more than two Project Pitches during a 12-month period;
- and may submit no more than three total Project Pitches for the same project, regardless of topic or timeline.

Therefore:

Do not burn a Project Pitch on a rushed or poorly supported submission.

The pitch itself is a limited strategic asset.

## 14. CURRENT CANDIDATE NSF TECHNICAL OBJECTIVES

These are working research directions and must be tested against the actual code and architecture before being treated as final.

### OBJECTIVE A — Adaptive Access Normalization

Research whether heterogeneous accessibility inputs can enter one common system without sacrificing latency, fidelity, contextual meaning, reliability, concurrency, or fallback behavior.

Potential inputs include typed interaction, touchscreen, speech, symbols, switch control, AAC systems, eye-gaze systems, and other supported accessibility interfaces.

### OBJECTIVE B — Adaptive Intervention State Engine

Research whether Respawn can dynamically transition among intervention/support states while remaining predictable, auditable, configurable, safe, explainable, and subject to educator/human oversight.

Potential measurements include false escalation, missed escalation, override rate, transition accuracy, recovery behavior, and traceability.

### OBJECTIVE C — Cross-Environment Adaptive Architecture

Research whether adaptive functionality can operate consistently across different hardware, accessibility configurations, user profiles, institutional environments, and deployment environments without requiring a separate application fork for every implementation.

## 15. REQUIRED R&D FORMAT

Every final NSF technical objective must eventually be expressed as:

- HYPOTHESIS — What do we believe is technically possible?
- TECHNICAL RISK — Why might this fail?
- METHOD — How will we test it?
- METRIC — What will we measure?
- SUCCESS THRESHOLD — What result constitutes success?
- FAILURE THRESHOLD — What result disproves or materially challenges the hypothesis?
- DELIVERABLE — What engineering/research artifact will result?
